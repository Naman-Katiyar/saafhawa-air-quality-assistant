    Indoor air hacks

    On smog days, indoor air decides most of your family's exposure. Start with the purifier size — most homes buy one that is too small.

    Enter your bedroom size and calculate — that is the room that matters most, because you spend 7–8 hours there every night.
    • Ventilate only in the clean window. Open windows during the day's cleanest stretch (shown above) for 20–30 minutes, then close them. Never ventilate early morning in winter smog season — that is usually the dirtiest air of the day.
    • Seal the gaps. Weather-strip or tape the gaps under doors and around window frames of the bedroom. On severe days this alone can cut indoor PM2.5 noticeably.
    • Wet-mop, don't dry-sweep. Dry sweeping throws settled dust back into the air. A damp mop and a damp cloth for surfaces keep particles down.
    • Pause the smoke sources. Agarbatti, dhoop, candles and mosquito coils add a heavy dose of PM2.5 in a closed room. Skip them on days when AQI is above 200.
    • Run the kitchen exhaust while cooking and for 10 minutes after. Gas and tadka fumes are a major indoor source that most families ignore.
    • Keep the purifier close and the door closed. A purifier cleans one room, not the whole flat. Place it near the bed, away from walls, and keep that room's door shut.
    • About indoor plants: they are good for mood, but you would need hundreds to match one small purifier. Spend on the purifier first.

    Low-emission routine swaps

    Small swaps your family can actually keep. Cleaner choices on high-AQI days also mean less time breathing traffic fumes.

    • Shift, don't cancel, the school run. If the school allows a slightly later drop on severe days, use the cleaner late-morning window instead of the 7 am smog peak.
    • Pool the commute. One car for three neighbours going the same way cuts both emissions and each person's roadside exposure time.
    • Choose the inside lane of the park. Trees and distance from the road edge measurably lower the PM you breathe during a walk.
    • Batch errands into the clean window. Vegetables, pharmacy and walks in one trip during the cleanest hours beats three trips through peak smog.
    • Keep two clean indoor evenings a week. Board games, indoor stretches and reading in the purified room replace the evening walk on the worst days — routine saved, lungs saved.
